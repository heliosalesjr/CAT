# Choosing the accent

Hero options D and E run on the same near-white sheet and the same terrain, and
differ in one thing: which colour carries the destination word and the primary
button. Both are in the tokens so the decision can be made against real screens.

## What was measured

| | on the sheet `#fbfcfd` | as a field, with text on it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `gold` `#f1bb4c` | **2.29:1** — fails as text | `ink` on gold: **7.61:1** |
| `gold-deep` `#d99f22` | 2.29:1 — fails as text | — |
| `green-deep` `#1f8a52` | **4.25:1** — passes for large text | — |
| `green-ink` `#177a47` | **5.23:1** — passes at reading size | `surface` on green-ink: **5.23:1** |
| `yellow` `#ffc107` | 1.52:1 — invisible | `ink` on yellow: **8.20:1** |

## Option D — gold

The logo's colour, kept exactly. It cannot be type on the sheet, so in D the
accent lives entirely in fills: the primary button, and nothing else. The
destination word is `ink`.

Lightening the sheet did not rescue it and was never going to. Gold measured
1.64:1 on the earlier neutral sheet and 1.71:1 on this one — both colours are
pale, and the gap between them is what matters, not how bright the ground is.
What the cooler sheet bought was hue separation: there is no warmth left in the
paper for the gold to dissolve into.

Gold's natural home is `navy`, where it reads 7.6:1 and finally looks like
struck metal. If the product keeps dark bands, gold can carry them whichever
option wins.

## Option E — green

The compass-ring green from the logo, at three depths because one green cannot
both sit on navy and carry text on the sheet. It takes the two things that carry
meaning — the destination and the primary button — and the vivid yellow drops to
punctuation: a filled chip on the country code, and `yellow-deep` on the eyebrow
hairline.

Green gains from the lighter sheet where gold could not, because it is a dark
accent: a lighter ground widens the tonal gap instead of closing it. The
destination went from 4.06:1 on the earlier sheet to 4.25:1, the button from
5.00:1 to 5.23:1.

## The recommendation

**Green, with gold reserved for navy grounds.** Not on taste — on what each one
can do. Green works in both directions, so a dashboard can use it for a KPI
figure, a link, a filled button and a chart stroke without changing value. Gold
can only ever be a field on light grounds, which means a dashboard built on it
needs a second colour for every piece of coloured type, and that second colour
becomes the real accent.

If gold stays the brand's signature, the honest form of that is option D's rule:
gold is for fills and for navy bands, and type stays in ink.
